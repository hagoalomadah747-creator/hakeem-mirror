About Hakeem

Last updated: 16 November 2026

What Hakeem is

Hakeem is an educational clinical case simulator for medical students, interns and doctors. Each case asks the learner to take the history, examine, stabilise, order investigations, treat and make the diagnosis, then shows a scored breakdown of every decision with a review article.

Who runs it

Hakeem is developed and run by Hago Azhari Elsheikh, Sudan. Website: hakeemsim.com. Contact: WhatsApp +249 91 098 1837.

Educational purpose

  • Practise clinical reasoning and emergency management on simulated patients.
  • Revise with a short article for every case and timed exams at five levels.
  • Prepare for licensing and specialty exams with past-exam style questions.

Intended users

Medical students, interns, residents and practising doctors. Hakeem is not intended for patients or the general public, and it is not directed to children.

Clinical review status

The cases and articles were written by the Hakeem team from published case reports. They have not yet been reviewed by an independent physician panel. Until a case is reviewed, treat its management steps and scoring as teaching material, not as clinical guidance. Reviewed cases will be marked in the app.

Medical disclaimer

Hakeem is for education only. It is not a medical device and must not be used to diagnose, treat or make decisions about real patients. Always follow local guidelines, your formulary and senior clinical advice.

Advertising and funding

Hakeem shows no advertising and has no sponsors. Content is not influenced by any company. Some content needs an access code.

Methodology & sources

1. Where the cases come from

Every case is adapted from a published, open-access case report in PubMed Central, licensed CC BY or CC BY 4.0. The full reference (authors, journal, year, PMC ID and DOI link) is shown in the article linked to each case, which opens from the case result and from the Articles tab.

Candidate reports and some figures were found with the MultiCaRe dataset (CC0 1.0):

  • Nievas Offidani M. (2025). MultiCaRe: an open-source clinical case dataset for medical image classification and multimodal AI applications (version 3) [Data set]. Zenodo. doi:10.5281/zenodo.10079369
  • Nievas Offidani M, Roffet F, González Galtier MC, Massiris M, Delrieux C. (2025). An open-source clinical case dataset for medical image classification and multimodal AI applications. Data, 10(8), 123. doi:10.3390/data10080123

2. What comes from the report, and what we add

  • From the report: the patient's presentation, history, key findings, investigation results and the final diagnosis.
  • Added by the Hakeem team: vital-sign trends and timings, the lists of possible actions, tests and drugs (including wrong choices), differential diagnoses, scoring, hints, and the teaching text of the articles.

Images come from the same open-access report unless stated otherwise.

3. How a case is scored

  • Each helpful action (examination, stabilisation, indicated test, correct drug, communication, handover) earns points; unneeded tests, wrong or harmful drugs and wrong diagnoses lose points.
  • The score is the share of the case's clinical points earned, so a complete and careful work-up reaches 100%. Speed does not change the clinical score; it only earns bonus experience points.
  • The result screen lists every decision with its points, the missed key actions and the wrong choices.

4. Difficulty and levels

  • Every case has a training level: student, intern, resident, fellow or attending.
  • Before a case the learner picks Easy (fewer wrong choices, hints, 4 diagnosis attempts), Medium (the case as written, 3 attempts) or Hard (look-alike diagnoses, wrong choices cost more, 2 attempts). The score is out of the same maximum at every difficulty.
  • Timed exams (50 questions, five levels) are built from the cases and articles; about 10% are Challenge questions from harder cases.

5. Limitations

  • Cases are simplified for teaching and follow one published patient; real patients vary.
  • Management may differ from your local guidelines and drug availability. Check doses in your formulary.
  • Rare and unusual presentations are over-represented in published case reports.

6. Technical description

The full methodology is published as an open technical note: Elsheikh, H. A. (2026). Hakeem: An Interactive Clinical Case Simulator Built from Open-Access Case Reports. Technical Description v1.0. Zenodo. doi:10.5281/zenodo.24301376

7. Corrections

If you find a medical error, please report it with the case name. We review every report and correct the case in the next content update.
